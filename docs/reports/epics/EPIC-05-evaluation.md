**How measured:** `source_hit@k`/`section_hit@k` are hit rates against `expected-spans-v1.json` (lenient = matches
any expected or acceptable-alternate source/section; strict = the exact expected one only), computed from
`records[*].retrieved` — no judge involved. `evidence_hit@k` additionally requires the retrieved chunk's char range to
overlap an expected evidence span, not just the right section.

**Observation:** retrieval is near-ceiling by k=5 (`source_hit@5` 0.953, `section_hit@5` 0.938 on both readings) but
loses ground at k=1, and lenient/strict diverge most there (`section_hit@1` 0.828 vs 0.750) — the top-ranked chunk is
in the expected section only 75 % of the time under the strict reading, though it is almost always the expected
*source*. `evidence_hit@1` (0.500) is much lower than `section_hit@1` (0.828): getting the right heading at rank 1
does not mean the top chunk's own text span overlaps the specific evidence sentence, which matters for k=1-only
consumers even when `any_evidence_hit@1` (0.875, any of the top-1 chunk's alternates) is healthier. The duplicate rule
had nothing to change (0/64) because, per EVAL-004a's read-only check, every duplicate pair's twin ranked 6–15 in
both arms — below the top-5 cut, so no kept chunk ever needed de-duplication against a dropped one. Gate refusals
land only on the two answerable VI cases (Q-EVAL-016, Q-EVAL-018, both arms) plus Q-EVAL-001 on arm A only — the same
question's arm B retrieval scored above threshold, so the gate's 0.686 cut is sensitive to per-arm chunking, not the
question itself.

**How measured:** per answered answerable record the judge scores each required answer point `yes`/`partial`/`no`,
flags contradiction of ground truth and unsupported claims; `metrics.mapping.map_result` turns that into one of
`correct` / `partially_correct` / `incorrect` / `false_refusal` (a gate or LLM refusal on an answerable question).
`accuracy` counts only `correct`; `lenient_accuracy` also counts `partially_correct`. Denominator is every *labelled*
answerable record (63 = 64 answerable − 1 unlabelled, Q-EVAL-002:B); Q-EVAL-002:B is not in `expected-spans-v1.json`'s
answerable-outside-coverage sense — it is simply judge_error, and is listed, not guessed.

**Observation:** the 17.5-point gap between lenient (90.5 %) and strict (73.0 %) accuracy is `partially_correct`
records missing one required point — e.g. Q-EVAL-030 (VI, both arms, `points_covered` 0.250, the lowest in the set)
and Q-EVAL-021 (EN, both arms, 0.500). `false_refusal` (6 records: Q-EVAL-001:A, Q-EVAL-016:A/B, Q-EVAL-018:A/B,
Q-EVAL-022:A) counts against both accuracy readings; 5 of the 6 are VI, which is most of the EN/VI accuracy gap below
("Per language"). `groundedness_rate` (0.965, 55/57) and `points_covered_mean` (0.927) show the answers that *are*
labelled are mostly well-supported when they answer at all — the shortfall is refusing or partially covering, not
fabricating.

**How measured:** per-stage wall-clock time recorded by the runner/judge at call time (`latency_ms` on each record/

**Observation:** the answer model's `generate` stage dominates and has a very heavy tail: p50 1510.4 ms vs. p95
36583.7 ms vs. max 42441.8 ms (`total`'s own mean, 3648.0 ms, sits below `generate`'s 4053.8 ms mean because `total`
also averages in the 11 gate-refused records, which skip generation entirely). Checked against the raw records
(not a guess): the tail is the runner's own client-side per-minute request throttle, folded into the `generate`
timer rather than reported separately — e.g. Q-EVAL-014:B's `generate` (42441.8 ms) breaks down as `throttle_wait`

cap, not a retried failure. Their paired arm (e.g. Q-EVAL-014:A at a normal 1954.8 ms) was unaffected, since only
calls queued behind others inside the same run's per-minute window pay the wait. The judge shows the same mechanism
attributed to a different stage by its own instrumentation: `generate` is tight (p50 1630.6 ms, p95 2065.1 ms, max

13 RPM cap) is counted in `total` only, not folded into `generate`. Both tails are deterministic self-throttling to
stay under the free-tier per-minute request budget, not provider-side variance.

## Measurement limitations

- **n ≈ 36** (32 answerable + 4 unanswerable; 63 labelled-answerable data points once split across 2 arms): each
  individual case is worth **≈ 1.6 pp** of an accuracy rate at n=63, or **≈ 2.8 pp** at n=36 per arm — small movements
  in any headline number can be one or two cases flipping, not a real capability change.
- **Single run per arm.** Each of the 36 cases was answered and judged exactly once per arm (EVAL-004a "Eval
  firewall") — there is no repeat-run variance estimate for any number in this report, so apparent per-case latency
  or answer differences between arms could partly reflect one-shot model non-determinism rather than a true
  chunking-scheme effect (EXP-001, EPIC-06, addresses this with paired statistics across the same 36 questions).
  Any per-arm figure pulled directly from this report's committed `summary.json` (e.g. `breakdown/arm/A/answer/
  accuracy` = 23/32 = 0.719) is an **unpaired**, single-run-per-arm figure, and will legitimately differ from
  EPIC-06's **paired** McNemar table (Arm A accuracy 22/31 = 0.710): EPIC-06 drops cases with no same-question
  partner in the other arm (`Q-EVAL-002:A` is correct but has no B partner). This is not an inconsistency between the
  two reports — it's the paired vs. unpaired denominator.
- **Self-grading bias.** The judge is the same model family as the answer model (`gemini-3.5-flash-lite` both sides),
  which risks the judge favouring its own answer style (`evaluation-spec.md` § Limitation). Mitigated, not removed,
  by the owner's blind spot-check above (n=10, 8/10 rule-based agreement) — a small sample of a systemic risk, not
  proof the risk is absent across the other 49 judged records.
- **Free-tier latency noise.** Two distinct effects are visible in "Latency" and neither is provider error: (a) the
  answer/judge `generate` tail (up to 42.4 s) is this project's own client-side per-minute request throttle, not
  provider slowness — see "Latency" observation; (b) `embed_query` itself still shows real network-call variance
  unrelated to throttling — its p50 is 0.2 ms (embedding-cache hits, no network call) against a p95 of 405.4 ms and a
  max of 2345.6 ms (actual live Gemini embedding calls, arm A's 36 cache misses) — an over 10× spread between
  individual live calls that free-tier rate limiting or regional routing could plausibly cause, not confirmed further.
- **VI detection limits.** `application/common/language.py::detect_language` is a heuristic: it labels text `vi` only
  if it contains a Vietnamese-specific letter or a Vietnamese tone mark, so Vietnamese text typed without diacritics
  would be misclassified as `en` (both the per-record `language` field and, live, the language the answer/refusal
  message is generated in — `answer_question.py` calls it on the raw question text). Every VI question in
  `eval-v1.jsonl` uses full Vietnamese diacritics, so this limitation did not affect this run's 18/18 EN/VI split,
  but it means the per-language breakdown reflects the *dataset's* language, not necessarily what a differently-typed
  real user question would trigger.
